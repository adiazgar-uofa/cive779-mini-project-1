Damage by Region

CIV E 779 · Mini-project 1 · Total probability with n groups

Where do damaged elements come from?

An inspector checks bridges, beams and buildings that come from n regions, each with its own damage rate. The theorem of total probability gives the chance that a randomly chosen element is damaged; Bayes' theorem tells which region a damaged element most likely came from.

From total probability to Bayes

1 · Total probability: P(D ∩ Rᵢ) and P(D)

Theory: P(D | Rᵢ)·P(Rᵢ)P(D), sum of the barsSimulated

Each bar is the probability that an element is damaged and comes from region i: P(D ∩ Rᵢ) = P(D | Rᵢ)·P(Rᵢ). These are the red areas of the mosaic below. Added together they give P(D), the last bar. Simulated value = damaged elements from region i ÷ N.

2 · Bayes: P(Rᵢ | D)

Theory: P(D ∩ Rᵢ) / P(D)Sum = 1Simulated

Probability that a damaged element came from region i. Each bar is the matching bar of chart 1 divided by P(D), so these always add up to 1. Simulated value = damaged elements from region i ÷ all damaged elements.

Column P(D∩Rᵢ) adds up to P(D). Column P(Rᵢ|D) adds up to 1.
